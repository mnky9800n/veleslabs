Open Subsurface Data

In preparation

A documented collection of openly licensed subsurface data, gathered from public archives and prepared for machine learning. The first release is 3D post-stack seismic.

Release 1: 3D post-stack seismic

Every migrated 3D post-stack volume we can find that is free to download and free to pass on. Today these sit in thousands of separate zip files across national archives, with inconsistent header layouts and little metadata. We collect them, check them, and describe each one.

About 70 TB of SEG-Y in all, plus the WAPIMS volumes still to be measured. We include full stacks, partial angle stacks and second migrations. Velocity models and pre-stack gathers are not part of this release. Sources that only allow non-commercial use or limit passing data on are left out.

Progress

We are downloading from the original archives now. As of 19 October 2026, 88 NAMSS surveys (1.5 TB), 116 SARIG surveys (0.6 TB) and 12 Cooper Basin 2D-cubed volumes (0.3 TB) are checked and stored: about 2.4 TB of SEG-Y.

Why

The seismic foundation models that transfer best today learned from Norwegian shelf data held in DISKOS. That data is often called free. In practice a company outside oil production pays about NOK 190,000 (about USD 20,000) a year for membership, plus data fees, and commits to three years. Released data carry no open license. No open training set of that size exists. This collection is a start on one.

Wells tied to the seismic

For the Gulf of Mexico we add 15,136 exploration wells from public tables of the Bureau of Ocean Energy Management, each with its result. Of these, 4,895 were drilled inside a NAMSS 3D survey after it was shot, and 1,233 of those found pay. Digital logs for 1,853 of the 4,895 wells (8,031 files, about 33 GB) are requested from the Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement. All are public US government releases.

What we add

  • Checked copies. Every file is verified against the checksum stored in its source archive and kept byte for byte, with a SHA-256 recorded for each.
  • Per-volume metadata. A metadata.json beside each volume gives inline and crossline ranges, number of samples, sample interval, the trace header byte locations that actually work, the coordinate reference system where the headers state one, and any header problems found.
  • A manifest. One CSV row per volume with source, survey, location, stack type, size, license, download URL and checksums.
  • A cloud-ready copy. Each volume will also be offered in MDIO (chunked Zarr), so a slice can be read without fetching the whole file. The SEG-Y originals stay as published.

Uses

Pretraining and testing seismic foundation models, comparing how models transfer between basins, and seismic interpretation research. We will publish a notebook that reads a volume straight from cloud storage and runs a pretrained seismic foundation model on it.

Get it yourself

A command-line tool and a Claude skill will fetch any part of the collection from the original archives, check every file and write the same metadata. Both will be on GitHub.

Citing it

The index, metadata and well tables will get a DOI on Zenodo.

Coming later

2D seismic, starting with the 582 NAMSS 2D surveys, and open well logs from other national archives, under the same checks and metadata.
